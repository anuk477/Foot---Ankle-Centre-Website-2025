### Published detail

Acupuncture at the Foot & Ankle Centre is a safe, evidence-based treatment used to relieve pain, reduce inflammation, and support natural healing in the feet, ankles, and lower limbs. By inserting fine, sterile needles into specific acupuncture points, this therapy helps regulate pain signals, improve circulation, and encourage the body's own healing response.
Acupuncture is commonly used to manage conditions such as plantar fasciitis, heel pain, Achilles tendon pain, arthritis, nerve-related foot pain, muscle tightness, and chronic foot or ankle discomfort. It can be particularly beneficial for patients seeking a non-invasive, drug-free approach to pain management or those with persistent symptoms that have not responded fully to other treatments.
At the Foot & Ankle Centre, acupuncture is delivered by trained clinicians and is often integrated into a personalised treatment plan alongside exercise rehabilitation, manual therapy, shockwave therapy, and orthotics—ensuring a holistic approach to recovery and long-term foot health.

### What to expect / highlighted information

**What to expect:** We explain the process and tailor your care according to your comfort and goals.

### FAQs

**What is acupuncture?**
A therapeutic technique involving very fine needles inserted into specific points on the body to relieve pain, improve circulation, and support healing.

**What conditions can acupuncture help with?**
Plantar fasciitis, heel and arch pain, Achilles tendon pain, arthritis-related foot and ankle pain, nerve pain or sensitivity, muscle tightness and stiffness, and chronic foot and ankle pain.

**How does acupuncture work?**
It stimulates nerves, muscles, and connective tissue—boosting blood flow, reducing inflammation, releasing natural pain-relieving chemicals, and regulating pain pathways.

**Is acupuncture painful?**
Most feel little to no pain. Mild tingling, warmth, or a dull ache is normal and often relaxing.

**How many acupuncture sessions will I need?**
Some notice improvement after 1–2 sessions; chronic conditions may need a short course over several weeks.

**Is acupuncture safe?**
Yes—very safe when performed by trained clinicians using sterile, single-use needles. Side effects are rare and usually mild, such as temporary soreness or bruising.

**Can acupuncture be combined with other treatments?**
Absolutely. It pairs well with exercise rehabilitation, myofascial release, trigger point therapy, shockwave therapy, and orthotics or gait correction.

**Is acupuncture suitable for everyone?**
It is suitable for most people. Your clinician will review your medical history to confirm appropriateness.

**How long does an acupuncture session last?**
Typically 20–40 minutes, depending on the condition and whether it’s combined with other therapies.

**How do I book an acupuncture appointment?**
Book via the Foot & Ankle Centre website, phone, or online enquiry form. We’ll assess your symptoms and advise if acupuncture is suitable.

### Overview

In-depth biomechanical assessment to understand how your feet and lower limbs move during walking and activity. Helps diagnose causes of pain and informs tailored treatment plans.

### Key points

- Footwear and activity advice

### Published detail

Biomechanics at the Foot & Ankle Centre focuses on understanding how your feet, ankles, and lower limbs move and function during everyday activities. A biomechanical assessment identifies abnormal movement patterns, alignment issues, muscle imbalances, and gait abnormalities that may contribute to pain or injury.
Using advanced gait analysis technology, pressure-mapping systems, and expert clinical examination, our specialists evaluate how your body bears weight, distributes pressure, and adapts while standing, walking, and running. This allows us to diagnose conditions such as overpronation, flat feet, high arches, tendon dysfunction, plantar fasciitis, knee pain, and recurrent sports injuries.
Following a detailed assessment, we create a tailored treatment plan which may include custom orthotics, footwear recommendations, strengthening programmes, physiotherapy, or referral for further imaging. At the Foot & Ankle Centre, our goal is to correct faulty biomechanics, reduce pain, improve function, and help you move with greater comfort and confidence.

### What to expect / highlighted information

**What to expect:** A gentle assessment with clear guidance and practical steps to improve comfort and performance.

### FAQs

**What is a biomechanical assessment?**
A biomechanical assessment is a detailed evaluation of how your feet and lower limbs function. It examines posture, alignment, joint mobility, muscle strength, and gait patterns to identify the root cause of pain or dysfunction.

**Who should have a biomechanical assessment?**
You may benefit if you have foot, arch, or heel pain; knee, hip, or lower back discomfort; recurrent sports injuries; flat feet or high arches; overpronation or supination; uneven shoe wear; or pain when walking/standing for long periods. Athletes and active individuals benefit greatly from optimisation.

**What happens during the assessment?**
Your clinician reviews your history, checks joint mobility and muscle strength, analyses posture and alignment, performs gait analysis using video or pressure-plate systems, and evaluates footwear to pinpoint causes of symptoms.

**What conditions can biomechanics help diagnose?**
It can identify plantar fasciitis, Achilles tendon issues, flat feet/high arches, overpronation, bunions and toe deformities, knee/hip/lower-back pain linked to poor foot mechanics, and sports-related overuse injuries.

**What treatments are recommended after the assessment?**
Depending on findings: custom orthotics or insoles, strengthening and stretching programmes, footwear modification, physiotherapy, activity adjustments, and referral for imaging or specialist care if needed.

**Do I need imaging such as X-rays?**
Not always. Imaging is only recommended if structural deformities, arthritis, or tendon injuries are suspected.

**Are custom orthotics part of the process?**
If abnormal mechanics are detected, custom-made orthotics are often advised to support alignment, reduce strain, and improve function.

**Is the assessment painful?**
No. It is non-invasive and pain-free, involving observation, gentle movement testing, and gait analysis.

**How long does the assessment take?**
Typically 30-45 minutes, covering examination, gait analysis, and discussion of recommended options.

**How do I book a biomechanics appointment?**
Contact the Foot & Ankle Centre via our website, phone, or online enquiry form. Our team will guide you through the next steps.

**What to expect:** Step-by-step support from assessment through to aftercare, helping you return to comfortable walking.

### FAQs

**What is bunion correction surgery?**
Bunion correction surgery realigns the bones and soft tissues around the big toe joint to remove the bunion bump, straighten the toe, and restore proper foot mechanics. It may involve bone cuts, joint realignment, or fusion, depending on severity.

**What symptoms indicate I may need bunion surgery?**
You may benefit if you have persistent pain, difficulty finding comfortable shoes, toe crowding or overlapping, swelling or redness, restricted movement, or a bunion that continues to worsen. A consultation will confirm if surgery is appropriate.

**What types of bunion surgery are available?**
Options include minimally invasive (keyhole) bunion surgery, Chevron and Scarf osteotomies, Lapidus fusion, and soft tissue correction. Your surgeon will recommend the best technique based on your bunion’s severity, foot structure, and lifestyle goals.

**Is bunion correction painful?**
The procedure is performed under local or general anaesthetic, so you won’t feel pain during surgery. Mild discomfort is expected during recovery but is manageable with standard pain relief.

**How long is the recovery period?**
Most patients can walk in a specialised post-operative shoe immediately. Typical recovery: 2–6 weeks reduced swelling/early mobility; 6–12 weeks return to most daily activities; 3–6 months full recovery. Timelines vary by technique.

**Will my bunion come back?**
Recurrence is uncommon when structural alignment is fully corrected. Our surgeons address the underlying bone and joint mechanics to ensure long-lasting stability.

**What are the risks of bunion surgery?**
Risks include infection, stiffness, swelling, nerve irritation, or recurrence. Your surgeon will explain these and take steps to minimise them.

**Is minimally invasive bunion surgery an option for me?**
Many patients with mild to moderate bunions are candidates for minimally invasive techniques, which use smaller incisions, reduce soft-tissue disruption, and promote faster healing with minimal scarring.

**Can I drive after bunion surgery?**
Driving is typically safe once you can walk comfortably and control the pedals—usually after 4–6 weeks, depending on which foot was operated on. Confirm with your surgeon.

**How do I book a consultation?**
Contact the Foot & Ankle Centre via our website, call the clinic, or submit an online enquiry. Our team will arrange a personalised assessment and guide you through your options.

### What to expect / highlighted information

**What to expect:** Gentle, quick treatment by a registered podiatrist with immediate comfort gains for most patients.

### FAQs

**What is a corn?**
A corn is a small, hard area of thickened skin that forms due to pressure or friction. Corns often appear on the tops of toes, between toes, or on weight-bearing areas of the foot.

**What causes corns to develop?**
Common causes include tight or narrow footwear, toe deformities (hammertoes, claw toes), bunions, long or overlapping toes, abnormal foot mechanics, and high-pressure areas when walking.

**How is a corn removed?**
A clinician gently removes the corn using sterile podiatric instruments. This is a quick, painless procedure that provides immediate relief.

**Does corn removal hurt?**
No. Corn removal is typically painless because the corn itself is dead skin. Most patients feel instant improvement.

**How long do results last?**
It depends on whether the underlying cause is addressed. If friction or pressure continues, the corn may return. We provide long-term strategies to reduce recurrence.

**Can corns come back?**
Yes-especially if caused by bone pressure or footwear rubbing. We may recommend custom orthotics, footwear modifications, protective cushions, toe separators, or surgical options for severe deformities to reduce recurrence.

**Do I need surgery for corns?**
Most corns do not require surgery. If structural problems such as bunions or hammertoes cause persistent corns, surgery may be considered for permanent correction.

**Is corn removal safe for diabetic patients?**
Yes-but only when performed by a trained clinician. Diabetic patients should avoid self-treatment due to increased risk of infection or skin injury.

**How often should I have corns removed?**
This varies. Some patients need maintenance every 6-12 weeks, while others only require occasional treatment once underlying causes are addressed.

**How do I book a corn removal appointment?**
Contact the Foot & Ankle Centre via our website, phone, or online enquiry form. Our clinicians will assess the corn, remove it safely, and advise on preventing future problems.

**What to expect:** We'll recommend the right product and fit for your needs with practical tips for daily wear.

### FAQs

**What are foot cushions used for?**
They reduce pressure, friction, and impact on sensitive areas of the foot. Common uses include corns, calluses, bunions, metatarsalgia, heel pain, and general discomfort from standing or walking.

**What types of foot cushions are available?**
Gel cushions for shock absorption, metatarsal pads for ball-of-foot pain, toe separators and protectors, bunion pads to reduce rubbing, heel cushions for heel pain or plantar fasciitis, and felt or foam padding for custom offloading. Your clinician will advise the best option.

**Who can benefit from using foot cushions?**
People with painful corns or calluses, pressure from bunions or hammer toes, forefoot pain or metatarsalgia, heel pain, fatigue from long periods on their feet, or friction/rubbing from footwear. Suitable for adults, athletes, and older adults.

**Are foot cushions a substitute for orthotics?**
They provide relief but do not correct underlying structural problems. Custom orthotics may be recommended for long-term biomechanical correction, while cushions offer immediate support and comfort.

**Can foot cushions be used with any shoes?**
Most fit easily into everyday shoes, trainers, and work footwear. Slimline or low-profile options are available for tighter shoes.

**How long do cushions for feet last?**
Gel cushions often last several months; felt or foam are shorter-term (sometimes single-use); silicone protectors can be long-lasting with proper care.

**Are foot cushions reusable?**
Many gel and silicone cushions are reusable and washable. Felt and foam pads are often single-use or short-term.

**Can cushions help prevent corns and calluses?**
Yes. They reduce pressure and friction that lead to skin thickening and work best when combined with proper footwear and regular podiatry.

**Are cushions suitable for people with diabetes?**
They can be helpful, but diabetic patients must be assessed by a clinician to ensure the product is safe, as foot sensitivity and skin integrity need special care.

**How do I get foot cushions from the Foot & Ankle Centre?**
Book an appointment or visit the clinic for assessment. Our clinicians will recommend the most suitable cushions and ensure they fit correctly for maximum comfort and relief.

### Overview

Diagnostic injections help identify the exact source of pain and guide the most effective treatment.

### Key points

- Consultation and explanation
- Targeted testing approach
- Clear next steps

### What to expect / highlighted information

**What to expect:** Discuss the findings with your clinician to decide on the right treatment plan.

### FAQs

**What are diagnostic injections?**
They involve placing a local anaesthetic into a specific structure of the foot or ankle to temporarily block pain. If symptoms improve, it confirms that area as the source of pain.

**Why would I need a diagnostic injection?**
They are used when pain is difficult to localise or when multiple structures could be responsible. Diagnostic injections guide accurate treatment decisions.

**What conditions can diagnostic injections help assess?**
Forefoot pain (e.g., Morton’s neuroma or joint pain), ankle arthritis, tendon or ligament pain, nerve entrapment, post-injury or post-surgical pain, and chronic unexplained foot or ankle pain.

**How is the injection performed?**
The clinician uses assessment and often ultrasound guidance to place a small amount of local anaesthetic into the suspected area for accuracy and safety.

**Is the procedure painful?**
Discomfort is minimal. A fine needle is used and the procedure is quick; most patients tolerate it very well.

**How long do the effects last?**
The numbing effect typically lasts a few hours and is used to confirm diagnosis rather than provide long-term pain control.

**What happens after the injection?**
You may be asked to walk, stand, or perform movements that usually cause pain. Your response helps confirm whether the injected structure is responsible.

**Are diagnostic injections the same as steroid injections?**
No. Diagnostic injections usually contain local anaesthetic only; steroid injections are therapeutic and designed to reduce inflammation over a longer period.

**Are diagnostic injections safe?**
Yes. Risks are very low and may include temporary soreness, bruising, or mild swelling. Ultrasound guidance further reduces risk.

**How do I book a diagnostic injection appointment?**
Book via the Foot & Ankle Centre website, phone, or online enquiry form. Our specialists will assess whether a diagnostic injection is appropriate for your condition.

They spread through direct contact, especially in warm, moist environments such as gyms, swimming pools, and changing rooms. They can also spread from one area of your own foot to another.

If it is painful or affects walking, has persisted for months, has failed over-the-counter treatments, involves multiple or mosaic verrucae, or if you have diabetes or circulation problems.

**What verruca treatments do you offer?**
Cryotherapy (freezing), acid treatments (salicylic or other medical-grade acids), Swift® microwave therapy, needling to stimulate an immune response, electrocautery, and surgical excision for persistent cases. Your clinician will recommend the ideal option.

**How many treatments will I need?**
It depends on the size, depth, and number of verrucae. Some clear in a single session; others need a short course over several weeks.

**Can verrucae come back?**
Yes. Because they are viral, they can recur if the virus remains active. Advanced treatments like Swift® and needling help stimulate the immune response to reduce recurrence.

**Are verruca treatments safe for children?**
Yes. Many treatments are suitable for children; the clinician will tailor the method to age, comfort, and severity.
